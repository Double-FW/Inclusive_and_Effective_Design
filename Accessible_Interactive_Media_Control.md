# Accessible Interactive Media Control  
## Human-Centred Requirements for Inclusive Television and Environmental Interfaces  

This document synthesises core themes, approaches, and outcomes derived from user‑centred development of accessible interactive media control systems.

All references to organisations, research bodies, institutions, named individuals, and study data have been removed.  
All content is expressed as abstract, transferable, human‑centred requirements suitable for inclusion in a GPT knowledge base.

---

# THEME 1 — Accessibility Must Be Participatory, Not Assumed

## Core Insight

Designing for people with complex motor, cognitive, sensory, and communication needs cannot be done through assumption.  
Lived experience must shape requirements, interaction models, and iteration cycles.

## Approaches

- Use participatory design methods.
- Test prototypes in real environments, not laboratory simulations.
- Adapt communication methods to suit individuals.
- Involve caregivers and support networks as facilitators, not decision-makers.
- Treat the technology as the subject of testing — not the person.

## Outcomes

- Interfaces reflect actual user capability.
- Reduced abandonment of assistive technologies.
- Higher long-term adoption and satisfaction.

# THEME 2 — Access Needs Are Multidimensional

## Core Insight

Motor limitations cannot be isolated from:

- Cognitive processing
- Visual perception
- Fatigue patterns
- Medication effects
- Communication constraints
- Social living arrangements

Accessibility must consider the whole person.

## Approaches

- Avoid designing solely for a single impairment category.
- Provide flexible interaction paradigms.
- Account for tiredness, fluctuating ability, and context of use.
- Design for shared and private environments differently.

## Outcomes

- More resilient systems.
- Reduced cognitive overload.
- Sustainable real-world usability.

---

# THEME 3 — Simplicity Is a Survival Requirement

## Core Insight

If configuration, maintenance, or repair is complex, systems will not be used.

Simplicity supports independence not only for users, but also for families and caregivers.

## Approaches

- Minimise setup steps.
- Reduce reliance on cables and fragile physical connections.
- Enable wireless or simplified hardware configurations.
- Avoid solutions that require frequent technical recalibration.
- Ensure systems can be reset or recovered easily.

## Outcomes

- Lower technical failure rates.
- Increased family adoption.
- Reduced reliance on specialist environments.

---

# THEME 4 — Cause and Effect Must Be Immediate

## Core Insight

Strong cause‑and‑effect relationships are essential for users who rely on scanning, dwell selection, or head‑controlled input.

Latency breaks trust.

## Approaches

- Minimise system response time.
- Provide visible confirmation of input.
- Ensure state changes are obvious and consistent.
- Avoid silent failures.
- Avoid requiring excessive sequential steps for core actions.

## Outcomes

- Increased confidence.
- Reduced frustration.
- Lower cognitive effort.

---

# THEME 5 — Personalisation Is Essential

## Core Insight

No two users have identical motor control, visual acuity, timing precision, or cognitive processing speed.

Fixed interfaces exclude.

## Approaches

- Provide adjustable scanning speeds.
- Allow configurable dwell times.
- Enable layout modification.
- Support user profiles for shared environments.
- Allow adjustable contrast and font size.
- Enable discrete volume steps rather than incremental micro-adjustments.

## Outcomes

- Greater autonomy.
- Improved comfort.
- Long-term engagement.

---

# THEME 6 — Interaction Paradigms Must Be Flexible

## Core Insight

Switch access, head control, touch, mouse, and joystick scanning each require different design logic.

One scanning pattern does not suit all users.

## Approaches

- Support single-switch autoscan.
- Support user-controlled scan progression.
- Support multi-switch directed scanning.
- Allow hierarchical grouping of options.
- Minimise deep menu navigation.
- Reduce abstraction where possible.

## Outcomes

- Increased inclusion across ability spectrum.
- Reduced training time.
- Lower interaction fatigue.

---

# THEME 7 — Cognitive Load Must Be Actively Managed

## Core Insight

Sequential thinking, abstraction, and multi-step navigation may present barriers for some users.

Complex menus reduce access.

## Approaches

- Limit options per screen.
- Use recognisable visual symbols.
- Prefer channel logos and images where appropriate.
- Avoid deep navigation tiers.
- Use overlays instead of full page transitions where possible.
- Preserve predictable layout structures.

## Outcomes

- Faster task completion.
- Reduced confusion.
- Greater independence.

---

# THEME 8 — Shared Environments Require Different Design

## Core Insight

Shared living spaces introduce competing preferences, privacy concerns, and social dynamics.

Accessibility must balance individual control with communal experience.

## Approaches

- Enable profile-based favourites.
- Allow dimming for shared rooms.
- Support private bedroom usage.
- Consider non-stigmatising aesthetics.
- Design interfaces that resemble mainstream consumer products.

## Outcomes

- Increased dignity.
- Reduced stigma.
- Social inclusion rather than isolation.

---

# THEME 9 — Assistive Systems Should Avoid Hardware Spoofing

## Core Insight

Systems that rely on replicating remote control signals or hardware chaining are fragile and difficult to maintain.

Open digital control interfaces are more sustainable.

## Approaches

- Prefer direct system APIs over infrared spoofing.
- Ensure dynamic data can be returned to the controller.
- Avoid one‑way control systems.
- Design for bi-directional communication.

## Outcomes

- Real-time feedback.
- Accurate state synchronisation.
- Reduced maintenance complexity.

---

# THEME 10 — Feedback Must Be Context-Aware

## Core Insight

Auditory feedback may be masked by programme audio.  
Visual feedback must be legible at distance.

Feedback must match context of use.

## Approaches

- Provide visual state indicators.
- Avoid relying solely on audio cues.
- Avoid subtle micro-changes in UI.
- Make navigation state visible.
- Provide confirmation when entering or exiting groups in scanning systems.

## Outcomes

- Lower error rates.
- Clearer interaction pathways.
- Increased usability across sensory profiles.

---

# THEME 11 — Technology Must Enable Agency

## Core Insight

Many individuals experience passivity when others control media or environment on their behalf.

Accessible control restores autonomy.

## Approaches

- Provide independent power control.
- Enable independent channel selection.
- Allow independent volume control.
- Support browsing and exploration.
- Avoid requiring a support worker for routine functions.

## Outcomes

- Increased confidence.
- Greater self-expression.
- Enhanced quality of life.

---

# THEME 12 — Accessibility Is an Ecosystem

## Core Insight

Accessible control interacts with:

- Environmental systems
- Communication aids
- Education tools
- Entertainment platforms

Integration matters.

## Approaches

- Design systems compatible with communication software.
- Enable integration with environmental controls.
- Avoid isolating media control from wider assistive ecosystems.
- Ensure extensibility.

## Outcomes

- Cohesive user experience.
- Reduced duplication of effort.
- Higher long-term sustainability.

# Cross-Cutting Design Principles

1. Design for variability.
2. Minimise latency.
3. Reduce abstraction where possible.
4. Prioritise personalisation.
5. Preserve dignity in aesthetic design.
6. Avoid over‑engineering.
7. Maintain strong cause‑and‑effect feedback.
8. Design for real environments, not controlled settings.
9. Enable agency.
10. Plan for maintainability.

---

# Executive Diagnostic Questions

- Where does latency undermine cause and effect?
- Which tasks require support worker intervention that could be autonomous?
- Is scanning customisable per user?
- Does the system degrade gracefully under fluctuating ability?
- Can families maintain it without specialist knowledge?
- Does the interface resemble mainstream products?
- Is feedback visible and immediate?
- Are personal profiles supported?
- Is cognitive load actively managed?
- Can the system evolve over time?
