# EAA Annex 1 — Training Dataset (Websites & Online Applications)

---

## 1. Core Accessibility Principles (Functional Interpretation)

### Perceivable  
Information must be presented in formats that users can process through one or more sensory channels. If information is not available in a processable format, it is not accessible. Content should be available in multiple modalities such as text, audio, or graphical representation, ensuring that the same information can be accessed in different ways depending on user need.

### Operable  
Interfaces must support interaction through a wide range of input methods, allowing users to operate systems using different tools and techniques. This includes compatibility with pointing devices, keyboards, touch interfaces, voice input, and alternative control mechanisms. Systems must allow users to control animations, adjust timing constraints, and recover from errors through forgiving interaction design such as warnings, confirmations, and cancellation options.

### Understandable  
Users must be able to comprehend both the information presented and the outcomes of their actions. Navigation and interaction patterns must be consistent, predictable, and logically structured. Processes such as form completion must include clear labels, guidance, and feedback, ensuring that users understand what is required and what has occurred as a result of their input.

### Robust (Universality)  
Digital services must function reliably across different platforms, devices, and technologies to support user choice. Systems should not restrict access by enforcing narrow technology requirements. Instead, they should enable interoperability and adaptability by following consistent development practices and ensuring that content can be consumed through a wide range of user-selected technologies.

### Customer Satisfaction (Risk Indicator)  
A service that does not deliver a satisfactory experience represents a failure of accessibility in practice, even if technical requirements appear to be met. Customer satisfaction should be treated as a measurable outcome aligned with accessibility performance, particularly when evaluated across different user groups.

## 2. User Group Requirements (Annex 1 Functional Coverage)

Each requirement must result in a usable and comparable experience for the following user groups:

### Usage without vision  
Systems must provide at least one mode of operation that does not require visual perception, ensuring that all functionality and information can be accessed without sight.

### Usage with limited vision  
Systems must support operation by users with reduced visual capability through features that enhance visibility or reduce dependency on fine visual detail.

### Usage without perception of colour  
Systems must not rely on colour perception alone to convey information or enable interaction, ensuring that all meaning is accessible without colour differentiation.

### Usage without hearing  
Systems must provide alternatives to auditory content so that all functionality and information can be accessed without hearing.

### Usage with limited hearing  
Systems must enhance auditory content or provide supplementary mechanisms to ensure that users with reduced hearing can effectively engage with the service.

### Usage without vocal capability  
Systems must not require spoken input as the only means of interaction, ensuring that all functionality can be accessed through non-vocal methods.

### Usage with limited manipulation or strength  
Systems must allow interaction without requiring fine motor control, high precision, or sustained physical effort, enabling use through alternative input methods or simplified interactions.

### Usage with limited reach  
Interactive elements must be accessible within a limited physical range, ensuring that users can operate the system without requiring extended reach or movement.

### Minimising risk of photosensitive seizures  
Visual content must avoid patterns or behaviours that could trigger seizures, ensuring safe operation for all users.

### Usage with limited cognition  
Systems must provide simplified modes of operation, clear guidance, and reduced complexity to support users with cognitive limitations, ensuring ease of understanding and use.

## 3. Information and Content Requirements (Annex 1 Section III)

### Multi-channel information access  
Information must be available through more than one sensory channel, ensuring that users can access content in different formats depending on their needs.

### Understandable presentation  
Content must be structured and written in a way that users can easily interpret, ensuring clarity of meaning and purpose.

### Perceivable presentation  
Content must be presented in formats that users can detect and process, ensuring that no essential information is hidden or inaccessible.

### Text-based availability  
Information must be available in text formats that can be transformed into alternative formats, enabling compatibility with a wide range of assistive and adaptive technologies.

### Readable visual presentation  
Text must use appropriate size, shape, spacing, and contrast to ensure readability. Presentation must allow adjustment to meet individual user needs.

### Alternative representations for non-text content  
All non-textual content must be accompanied by equivalent representations that communicate the same information in an accessible format.

### Consistent accessibility across systems  
Websites and applications must consistently apply the principles of perceivability, operability, understandability, and robustness across all content and functionality.

---

## 4. Product Information Requirements (Applied to Digital Delivery)

When product-related information is delivered digitally, it must:

- Be available through multiple sensory channels  
- Be presented in an understandable format  
- Be perceivable by all users  
- Use readable and adaptable visual presentation  
- Be available in text formats suitable for transformation  
- Include alternatives for non-text content  

These requirements ensure that digital instructions and product information are accessible in the same way as core service content.

---

## 5. Service-Level and Functional Requirements

### Accessible audiovisual delivery  
Accessibility features in media must be transmitted with sufficient quality, synchronisation, and user control to ensure effective use.

### Interoperability with assistive technologies  
Systems must function with a range of assistive technologies, including both software and hardware, ensuring that accessibility is not limited to specific configurations.

### Accessible identification, security, and payment processes  
All critical service functions, including authentication and transactions, must be usable and understandable for all users.

### Readable content complexity  
Information must be written at a level that is broadly understandable, avoiding unnecessary complexity and ensuring accessibility of meaning.

### Multi-modal communication support  
Where communication is required, systems must support multiple formats such as text, voice, and video to ensure inclusivity.

### Accessible support services  
Customer support channels must be accessible, providing users with multiple ways to seek assistance and resolve issues.

---

## 6. Design and Implementation Model (For AI Interpretation)

To demonstrate compliance, each requirement must be addressed through three aligned components:

### Technical Implementation  
The system must include the necessary features and behaviours that enable accessibility across different user needs.

### Inclusive Design Approach  
Design decisions must consider diverse user requirements from the outset, ensuring that accessibility is embedded rather than added retrospectively.

### User Outcome Evidence  
Accessibility must be validated through measurable outcomes, including whether users can complete tasks, understand content, and interact effectively. These outcomes must be analysable across the defined user groups.

---

## 7. Outcome-Based Success Criteria

For any system to be considered accessible, the following outcomes must be consistently achievable:

- Users are able to complete intended tasks successfully  
- Content layout supports efficient interaction and comprehension  
- Systems function effectively across different technologies  
- Time constraints do not prevent task completion  
- Users report a satisfactory and usable experience  

These outcomes must be validated across all relevant user groups to ensure equitable access.
